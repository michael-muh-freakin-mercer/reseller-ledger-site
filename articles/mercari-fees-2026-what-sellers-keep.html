Mercari fees 2026: what sellers actually keep

November 2026 · These are typical US rates — Mercari can change them, and your rate is whatever your receipt shows. Same defaults as our free fee calculator.

Mercari has the simplest seller fee of the big three marketplaces, and after a couple of well-publicised flip-flops it has settled: one flat 10% selling fee on the item price plus any shipping the buyer pays. There is no listing fee, no monthly plan, and — unlike eBay or Etsy — no separate payment-processing fee. The buyer pays their own 3.6% Buyer Protection fee on top, which does not come out of your payout. Simple. But two things catch sellers out: the fee applies to buyer-paid shipping too, and "free shipping" is often the more expensive choice.

Because the fee is a flat percentage with no fixed part, it takes the same share of every sale — $1 on a $10 item, $10 on a $100 item. That is good news for cheap stock: on a $5 item, eBay's ~13.6% plus a 30¢ fixed fee costs about 98¢, while Mercari costs 50¢. On low-value items Mercari is the cheapest of the big marketplaces to sell on.

Buyer-paid shipping is in the fee base

This is the part people miss. The 10% is charged on item price + buyer-paid shipping, not on the item alone. If the buyer pays $8 shipping, your fee base is the item plus $8. Worked example on a $40 item:

ScenarioFee baseMercari feePayout after label
Buyer pays $7.99 shipping$47.99$4.80$35.20
You offer free shipping ($7.99 label)$40.00$4.00$28.01

Payout = what lands in your balance minus the cost of the label. Direct deposit to your bank is free.

Look at the gap: the "free shipping" version leaves you $7.19 worse off on the same $40 sale. You save 80¢ of fee but pay the entire $7.99 label yourself. Mercari's own default is buyer-paid shipping, and unless you have deliberately built the postage into your price, offering free shipping quietly donates your margin to the shipping company.

What Mercari does not charge

  • No listing fee and no monthly subscription — you're only charged when an item sells.
  • No separate payment-processing fee. Mercari dropped it on 6 January 2025; before that it was 2.9% + 50¢. If a guide still quotes that, it's out of date.
  • No fee to get paid. ACH direct deposit is free (one per day). Only Instant Pay costs money — $3 flat per cash-out, up to $600 a month. On a $35 sale that $3 is 8.5% of your payout, so Instant Pay is the one add-on worth avoiding unless you need the cash today.

Two smaller charges to know

Cancellation fee. Sellers who cancel often pay 5% of the item price, capped at $25. It doesn't hit occasional cancels, but a listing you can't fulfil can cost you on both sides.

Label adjustments. If a prepaid label is underweighted, Mercari charges the difference to your balance. Weigh before you list — a "$7" label on a 4 lb box can come back as a surprise deduction.

Why you'll see contradictory numbers online

Mercari's US fee structure has changed twice in two years, so older articles are all over the place:

  • Before 27 March 2024: sellers paid 10% plus 2.9% + 50¢ processing.
  • 27 March 2024: Mercari scrapped the seller fee and put a service fee on buyers.
  • 6 January 2025 (current): the 10% seller fee came back, the buyer's processing fee was dropped, and buyers now pay a flat 3.6% Buyer Protection fee.

The rule of thumb: if a page quotes a 10% buyer fee, a 2.9% + 50¢ processing charge, or "no seller fee," it describes an older model. The current number is 10%, seller-paid, on item + buyer-paid shipping.

Mercari vs the others, in one line

Mercari's flat 10% beats Poshmark's 20% (or $2.95 flat under $15) on almost everything, and beats eBay's ~13.6% + 30¢ on cheap items — though eBay's larger buyer pool can win on reach for higher-priced stock. Mercari's real weakness isn't the percentage; it's that the 10% also skims buyer-paid shipping, so a shipping-heavy listing pays more.
