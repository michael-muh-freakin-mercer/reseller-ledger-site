How to track reseller inventory and profit in a spreadsheet (without losing your mind)

November 2026

Every reseller hits the same wall around 50–100 listings: the notes app is chaos, the "one big sheet" has become spaghetti, and nobody can answer the only question that matters — what did I actually keep? Here's a system that holds up, whether you build it yourself or buy it done.

The five things your tracker must capture

  • Item cost (COGS), per item. Not "I spent $200 at the bins" — which item got how much of that $200. Allocate haul totals across items the day you buy.
  • The fee the platform actually took. Per sale, not estimated per month. Fees differ wildly (Vinted 0% vs Poshmark 20%) and the difference is your profit.
  • Shipping, both sides. What the buyer paid and what the label cost you. The difference is often negative.
  • Days held. Date purchased vs date sold. Stock older than 90 days is where cash goes to die — it should trigger a price drop, not sit quietly.
  • Expenses and mileage. Poly mailers, tape, promoted listings, sourcing trips. Deductible and easy to forget.

The columns that work

For inventory: item · SKU · date purchased · source · category · qty · cost/unit · status · platform listed · date listed. For sales: date · SKU · platform · qty · sale price · shipping charged · shipping cost — and then let formulas compute COGS (look up the SKU), platform fee (look up the platform's rate), net profit and days-to-sell. Typing five things per sale is sustainable; typing twelve is not.

Bundles: the margin killer

A $45 bundle of two shirts is not "$45 of revenue." Split it: give each item a share of the total proportional to its solo value (a $25 shirt and a $20 shirt get 56% and 44%). Log one sales row per item with the allocated revenue and put the shipping cost on just one row. Otherwise your per-item margins are fiction and you'll keep making bundles that lose money.

The weekly habit that makes it work

Trackers fail from neglect, not design. The routine that sticks: log sales the day they ship (3 minutes), log sourcing the day you buy (photo the receipt, add the row), and once a week sort inventory by days-held and reprice anything over 90 days. That's it — roughly 15 minutes a week.

Tax time

If your COGS, fees, expenses and mileage are logged per-sale, the annual summary is a byproduct, not a project. Group expenses into Schedule C-style categories (supplies, advertising, software) from day one and your accountant — or FreeTaxUSA — will thank you.
