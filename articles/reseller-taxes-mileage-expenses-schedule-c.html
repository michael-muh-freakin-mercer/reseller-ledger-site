Reseller taxes without the panic: what to track all year so Schedule C is easy

November 2026

Most resellers don't fail at taxes because the rules are hard — they fail because the records were never kept. If your only number is "what hit my bank account," you've already lost money you're allowed to deduct. Here's what to capture during the year so filing is a byproduct, not a two-week archaeology project. (This is general information, not tax advice — your situation may differ, and a CPA is worth it the first year.)

The four buckets everything falls into

Sole-proprietor resellers report on Schedule C. Nearly every expense you'll ever have lands in one of four places, and knowing which is which is 90% of the battle:

COGS is not "everything I spent at the bins"

This is the single most common mistake. Inventory cost is deducted when the item sells, not when you buy it — so the hauls still sitting in your garage at year-end aren't deductible yet. That's why per-item cost matters: allocate a haul total across the items the day you buy (a $200 bin run that yields 20 items is $10/item), track it by SKU, and let the sale trigger the deduction. If you can't say what one item cost, you can't defend the deduction and you can't see your true margin either.

Mileage: the deduction resellers forget

Every sourcing trip, post-office run and photo shoot is a business mile. For 2026 the IRS standard business mileage rate is 72.5 cents per mile for Jan 1–Jun 30 and 76 cents per mile for Jul 1–Dec 31 (the IRS revised the rate mid-year as fuel prices rose). Charity miles are fixed at 14 cents. The only thing that makes this deductible is a log — date, destination, purpose, miles. A notes app beats nothing, but a mileage tab in your tracker that you fill in once a week beats a shoebox of receipts.

Small stuff you can just expense

Under the IRS de minimis safe harbor, most resellers can deduct equipment costing $2,500 or less (a label printer, a scale) in the year they buy it rather than depreciating it over years. Software subscriptions, storage units and a dedicated home-office space (if used regularly and exclusively) are ordinary expenses too. Keep receipts; categorize on the way in, not in April.

The weekly habit that saves your January

None of this works retroactively. Fifteen minutes a week does: log sales the day they ship (which computes COGS and fees automatically if your tracker is set up right), log sourcing the day you buy, and log the week's miles. Do that and your Schedule C is a report you print, not a puzzle you solve.
